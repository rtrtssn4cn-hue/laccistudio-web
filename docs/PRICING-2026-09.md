## 7. Etsy vs website strategy

1. **Keep identical prices** for now. No price-parity rule was found, but at current volume the website isn't cheaper to run (Snipcart minimum), and matching prices avoids undercutting Etsy trust.
2. Revisit once the website sells **over ~$1,000/month**. Then add a modest direct-site advantage, such as a free-shipping threshold or a gift-note option, rather than lower item prices.
3. **Never** put website coupons, QR codes or "buy direct" cards in Etsy packages. Etsy prohibits steering its buyers off-platform.
4. Use Etsy's **Share & Save** links in your own social posts that point to Etsy; they refund 4% of those orders.
5. Decide on Offsite Ads while opt-out is still possible (under $10k in any 365 days). At 15% it roughly doubles fees on every order it touches.
6. Retitle the coaster listings so the headline matches the default price, e.g. "Personalized Photo Ceramic Coaster — Single or Set of 4".

## 8. Costs we still need (flagged — not invented)

None of these exist in the project. A final price needs:
- Blank cost per unit (coaster, 20 oz tumbler, 11 oz mug, polyester tee by size)
- Ink and sublimation paper per print
- Tape, protective wrap, box and filler per order
- Electricity per press cycle (small, but part of per-unit cost)
- Labor minutes per item and the hourly rate you want to pay yourself
- Failed-print / reprint allowance (%)
- Actual packed weights and box sizes (the website values are estimates)
- Etsy shipping-profile settings (why coasters charge $8.07)
- Snipcart plan and actual Stripe/PayPal fees on your account
- Target profit margin

With these, each product gets a floor price (cost + fees + margin) to set against the market ranges above.
